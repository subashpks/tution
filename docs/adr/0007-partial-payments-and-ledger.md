# 0007 — Payments are an additive ledger, not columns on the invoice

**Status:** accepted · 2026-11-05

## Context

An invoice used to carry the whole money story in its own columns: `status`,
`paid_at`, `method`. That works for one full payment, but real academies take
money in parts — half now, half after payday — and they need to see the trail
afterwards. Squeezing partial payments into an invoice means storing a
remainder, rewriting it on every payment, and losing the history the moment
someone edits it.

## Decision

- Every payment is its own row in `payments`: `amount_paise`, `method`,
  `recorded_by`, `created_at`. The ledger only grows; a payment is never
  edited, only recorded.
- The invoice keeps its total (`amount_paise`). Its paid amount is the sum of
  its payments, and its status is derived: `paid` when the sum reaches the
  total, `part-paid` otherwise.
- `record_payment(p_invoice, p_amount_paise, p_method)` is the only write path
  to `payments`. It checks `is_admin()`, locks the invoice, rejects amounts
  above the outstanding, inserts the payment, and derives the status in the
  same transaction — so an invoice can never disagree with its ledger.
- Guardians can read the ledger rows of their own children's invoices; only
  the admin records payments. There is no client-side "edit payment".
- Monthly invoices are generated by the `generate-invoices` edge function on
  the 1st. A unique index on `(academy_id, student_id, period_label)` makes the
  job idempotent; the invoice number stays a per-year counter.

## Consequences

- History is safe: recording a payment never overwrites an earlier one.
- "Collected this month" and the last-6-months trend are simple sums over the
  ledger, not a snapshot column that can drift.
- The old `mark_invoice_paid` function remains for compatibility, but new code
  records payments through `record_payment`.

## Alternatives rejected

- **A `paid_paise` column on invoices**: one edit loses the history, and
  "who paid when, in which two parts" has nowhere to live.
- **Separate "installments" table managed by the client**: two write paths to
  the same money means two places to get the total right.
- **Letting tutors record payments**: money is the admin's job; the ledger
  should show exactly one hand.
